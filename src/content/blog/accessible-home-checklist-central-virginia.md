Listings rarely describe accessibility well. Here's a starter checklist to bring on tours.

## Getting in and around
- Number of steps at each entrance, and whether a ramp could fit
- Doorway and hallway widths
- A bedroom and full bathroom on the main level
- Flooring that works for wheelchairs, walkers, or kids who fall

## Sensory and safety
- Traffic and noise on the street at different times of day
- A fenced yard, or room to add one
- Lighting you can control, and a quiet room for a calm-down space
- Pools, stairs, or water nearby if your child wanders

## Daily life
- Drive times to school, therapy, and specialists at the hours you'd actually drive
- Which school division the home is in. Ask each division directly about IEP and 504 support.
- Room for medical equipment, supplies, or a caregiver

## Can it be adapted?
A home that isn't accessible today might be a good fit with changes. Get a contractor or occupational therapist's opinion before you make an offer.
